Local Food

Beyond its tourist spots, Banyuwangi also serves traditional dishes that are equally tasty and memorable.

Sego Tempong

Spicy

Sego Tempong is one of Banyuwangi’s most iconic dishes. It consists of warm rice served with boiled vegetables, fried tempeh or tofu, salted fish, and a fiery sambal that “tempong” — meaning slaps — your tongue with its spiciness. Simple yet powerful, this dish is loved for its authentic flavor and humble presentation.

Unique Feature: The sambal is freshly made and uniquely "Nempong" (slappingly spicy).

Pecel Pitik

Culture

Pecel Pitik is a traditional dish from the Osing tribe in Banyuwangi. It is made from free‑range chicken mixed with grated coconut and special herbs, creating a rich and aromatic flavor. Originally served during cultural ceremonies, today it has become a beloved local delicacy that represents Banyuwangi’s heritage.

Rujak Soto

Curry

Rujak Soto is a unique Banyuwangi dish that combines two Indonesian favorites: rujak cingur and soto. The savory soto broth blends with the sweet‑spicy rujak seasoning, creating a flavor that is bold, refreshing, and unlike anything else. This fusion makes Rujak Soto one of the most distinctive culinary experiences in Banyuwangi.

Best for: Anyone looking for a unique and bold flavor combination.

Sego Cawuk

Fresh

Sego Cawuk is a traditional Banyuwangi breakfast dish. It consists of rice served with various side dishes like pindang fish, boiled eggs, sambal, and vegetables, all topped with a special gravy made from grated coconut and spices. The combination creates a rich, savory, and slightly sweet flavor that locals love to start their day with.

Fun Fact: It is very affordable and a staple breakfast choice for locals.
